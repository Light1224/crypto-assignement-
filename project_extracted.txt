1.1 Motivation
•Quantum threat to classical cryptography.A sufficiently large quantum computer
running Shor’s algorithm can efficiently break RSA and ECC — the same number-theoretic
assumptions this course builds toward.
•Lattice problems resist quantum attack.The Shortest and Closest Vector Problems
(SVP/CVP) have no known efficient quantum algorithm, making lattice-based schemes
leading post-quantum candidates.
•Validated by real-world standards.NIST’s finalized post-quantum standards, Kyber
and Dilithium, are both lattice-based — this topic is directly aligned with where the
industry is heading.
•A direct replacement for RSA/ECC.LWE is a public-key primitive built on modular
linear algebra (A·s+emodq) — a quantum-safe swap-in for the asymmetric role RSA
and ECC play today.
1.2 Methodology
A self-contained 3-month roadmap, independent of the classical-cipher coursework track. A
single, self-contained primitive — not a full lattice library — kept to one clean deliverable per
month.
Month 1: Foundations
•Modular arithmetic & vector-space foundations for LWE.
•Study the core LWE relation:A·s+e(modq).
•Parameter scoping — hown,q,σtrade off security vs. correct decryption.
Month 2: Core Build
•Build toy-scale LWE key generation, encryption & decryption in Python.
•Package it all as a single, runnable Jupyter notebook.
•Sanity-check correct decryption at the chosen toy parameters.
Month 3: Analysis
•Plot empirical noise growth against theq/4decryption-failure threshold.
•Compile a theoretical key/ciphertext-size table vs RSA-2048/4096 & ECC-256.
•Write up findings in the notebook + a short report.

3.3 Why Ring-LWE Instead of Basic LWE
Basic LWE uses large matrices, making keys and ciphertexts enormous. Ring-LWE replaces
matrices with polynomials in the ringZ q[X]/(X n + 1), wherenis a power of two. This is
the foundation ofNewHope, the key-exchange protocol deployed by Google in Chrome, and
Kyber, NIST’s post-quantum KEM standard.
Ring-LWE is significantly more complex than basic LWE: you must implement polynomial arith-
metic modX n + 1, modular reduction, noise sampling from discrete Gaussian distributions, and
ideally the Number-Theoretic Transform for efficient multiplication. That complexity is exactly
what makes it appropriate for a 1-month, 3-person project.

6.1 Part 1: Why Quantum Computers Break RSA and ECC
6.1.1 1.1 Classical cryptography’s foundation
RSA relies on factoring a large numberN=p·q. ECC relies on the Elliptic Curve Discrete
Logarithm Problem (ECDLP). Both are hard for classical computers.
6.1.2 1.2 Shor’s algorithm
In 1994, Peter Shor showed that aquantum computercan solve both factoring and discrete
logarithm inpolynomial time.
•Classical: Best factoring algorithm takes sub-exponential time.
•Quantum (Shor): Takes roughlyO(n 3)time.
ThismeansRSA-2048couldbebrokeninhoursordaysonasufficientlylargequantumcomputer.
6.1.3 1.3 Why lattice problems are different
Lattice problems (SVP, CVP) haveno known efficient quantum algorithm. Shor’s algo-
rithm does not apply because factoring and discrete log have hidden algebraic structure that
quantum Fourier sampling exploits. Lattice problems do not appear to have this structure.
